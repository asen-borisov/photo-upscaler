PHOTO UPSCALER for Linux - how to install and use
=================================================

Makes your photos bigger and sharper with AI, for large prints or tight crops.
Everything happens on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded. No account. Free to use.

Works on: Ubuntu 22.04 or newer, Linux Mint 21 or newer, Fedora 36 or newer, Pop!_OS, Zorin,
and most other Linux versions from 2022 onwards.


INSTALL (one time, about 2 minutes)
-----------------------------------
1. Right-click "PhotoUpscaler-1.0.0-linux.tar.gz" and choose "Extract Here".
   You get a folder called "Photo Upscaler". Move it somewhere you'll keep it, for example
   your Home folder or Documents.
2. Open the folder. Right-click "Add to Applications menu.sh" and choose "Run as a Program".
   (On some systems: double-click it and choose "Run".)
   A message says "Done!".
3. Open your applications menu and search for "Photo Upscaler". Click it.

If step 2 doesn't work, open a Terminal inside the folder (right-click on empty space >
"Open in Terminal") and type:
    ./"Add to Applications menu.sh"
and press Enter.

Important: keep all the files together in the "Photo Upscaler" folder. If you move the folder
later, run "Add to Applications menu.sh" again.


USE IT
------
1. Drag photos (or a whole folder) into the window. Or click "Add photos".
2. On the right, choose:
     AI model    Natural photo       best for normal camera photos (start with this)
                 Soft or compressed  for phone pictures, web downloads, old scans
                 Quick preview       fast check, lower quality
     Output size Scale     2x makes the photo twice as wide and twice as tall
                 Pixels    an exact size, e.g. 6000 px on the longest side
                 Print     enter the print size in cm or inches, e.g. 60 cm at 300 dpi
3. Click "Upscale". Each photo shows its progress and the time left.
4. Click a finished photo to compare BEFORE and AFTER. Drag the white line left and right.
   Click on the picture to see it at 100% (real pixels). Drag to move around.
   Double-click (or "Fit") to see the whole photo again.
5. Click "Show in folder" to find the new file.

Where are my new photos?
   In a folder called "Upscaled" right next to your original photos,
   named like  IMG_0042_upscaled_2x.jpg
   (You can choose a different folder under "Save to" > "Change".)

Your originals are NEVER changed or replaced.
The new file keeps your colour profile, camera info, copyright and Lightroom keywords.


GOOD TO KNOW
------------
- It is slow on laptops without a graphics card. A photo can take several minutes, and a
  large camera file (20+ megapixels) can take 30 minutes or more on a basic laptop.
  Leave it running; you can keep working. "Quick preview" is about 25 times faster.
- "AI strength": if a photo looks too "processed", lower it to 70-80% for a more natural look.
- Works with JPEG, TIFF, PNG, WebP and iPhone HEIC files.
  RAW files (CR3, NEF, ARW...): export them from your photo editor first, then upscale the export.
- Maximum is 4x per run.
- Upscale the FINAL edited photo, after retouching, not before.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
- "The upscaling engine failed": the graphics driver needs Vulkan support.
  Ubuntu / Mint / Pop!_OS: open a Terminal and type
      sudo apt install mesa-vulkan-drivers libvulkan1
  Fedora:
      sudo dnf install mesa-vulkan-drivers vulkan-loader
  NVIDIA cards: install the "proprietary" NVIDIA driver (Ubuntu: Software & Updates >
  Additional Drivers).
- The app does not open: send this file to whoever set up the app for you:
      ~/.local/share/PhotoUpscaler/app.log
  (In the file manager press Ctrl+H to see hidden folders like .local)
